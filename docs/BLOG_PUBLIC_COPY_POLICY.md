# Blog Public-Copy Policy

This policy applies to every WECARE.DIGITAL blog audit, remediation pass, new article, republish, and publish verification for both Conversations and Gastronomy.

## Hard rule

Public blog copy must stand on its own. Do not expose source/reference scaffolding or external attribution in any public title, slug, SEO title, meta description, excerpt, or body.

The public copy must not contain:

- source author, teacher, philosopher, chef, researcher, public-figure, contributor, or other source-person names;
- source book, film, article, paper, study, report, publication, institution, publisher, organization, programme, course, or named teaching/framework references when they identify the source;
- citations, reference lists, source labels, source URLs, footnotes, "according to", "adapted from", "quoted from", "published by", or similar provenance/attribution language;
- source-style quotations whose purpose is to quote or credit an external person or work;
- private provenance phrases or source-biography details.

Internal provenance is allowed and encouraged in non-public metadata, ledgers, hashes, source profiles, and audit records. It must not leak into public copy.

## No attribution exception

There is no "required public attribution" exception in the blog pipeline. If material cannot be used without public attribution, do not publish that material in the blog copy. Reconstruct the underlying useful idea independently, omit the attributed passage, or leave the item for human review outside the publish queue.

## What may remain

Ordinary culinary, cultural, geographic, technical, or factual language may remain when it is part of the article's subject rather than source provenance. A source profile may explicitly allow ordinary public terms such as established dish names or culinary geography, but may not allow a source author, source publication title, publisher, institution, private person, or source-provenance phrase.

## Audit rule

Every audit must check the public surfaces for this policy before an editorial batch can be approved. A leak is a blocking finding. Do not repair it by deleting the post; preserve the record and propose a surgical public-copy edit.

## Preservation

This policy never authorizes deletion, retirement, or record-count reduction. The preservation floor remains 1,430 blog records: 976 Conversations and 454 Gastronomy.
